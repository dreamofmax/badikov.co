On collecting anonymous family photographs

Max Badikov — photography collector

Alongside new documentary work, there's an ongoing interest in anonymous found family photographs — pictures stripped of their original owners, context, and captions, and what they still manage to communicate once all of that is gone.

A found photograph has none of the framing a photographer usually gets to control: no artist statement, no series title, often not even a date. What's left is just the frame itself, and whatever it can carry on its own. That turns out to be a useful test for a documentary image generally — if a picture still reads decades after everyone who could explain it is gone, it was doing something right in the first place.

Collecting this kind of vernacular photography is a different discipline from shooting it. It's closer to editing: looking at thousands of ordinary frames to find the handful that hold up once stripped of context, in the same way a picture desk looks for the frame that holds up once stripped of a caption.

It's also a quiet argument against the idea that a documentary photograph needs a named author to matter. Plenty of the strongest ones in this collection don't have one, and never will.
