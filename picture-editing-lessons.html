Picture editing

What picture editing taught me about my own photographs

Max Badikov — picture editor

Years spent on the editing side — sequencing, captioning, and selecting other photographers' work for publication — change how you shoot your own. An editor learns to see a set of frames as a story rather than a collection of good shots, and that habit carries back into the field.

The best frame in a sequence is rarely the most technically perfect one; it's the one that earns its place next to the others. That's a hard thing to see in your own work while you're still shooting it, and editing experience is one of the few shortcuts to seeing it sooner.

It also changes the editing instinct itself. Knowing what a picture desk actually needs — a clean edit, a defensible caption, a frame that still reads at speed — makes it easier to look at your own contact sheet the way a stranger would, rather than the way you remember the moment.

The two sides feed each other constantly: shooting sharpens the eye an editor brings to someone else's work, and editing sharpens the discipline a photographer brings to their own.
